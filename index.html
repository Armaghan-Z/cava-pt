Physical Therapy 101

What is physical therapy?

Physical therapy is a healthcare specialty focused on restoring movement, reducing pain, and rebuilding strength after injury or surgery.

A physical therapist assesses how your body moves and designs a fully personalized plan using exercises, manual therapy, and education to restore full function.

For athletes, PT goes much further — it rebuilds not just the injured area but the entire kinetic chain, ensuring you return to sport with proper mechanics and dramatically reduced re-injury risk.

Every treatment targets the root cause. Whether it's a fractured bone, torn ligament, or chronic joint pain, therapy addresses the specific muscles, bones, and connective tissues involved — not just the symptoms you feel.

Key Clinical Vocabulary06 terms
ROM

Range of Motion — degrees a joint can move. Measured at every session to track healing progress precisely.

ORIF

Open Reduction & Internal Fixation — surgery that realigns broken bones using metal plates and screws.

FLEXION

Flexion / Extension: Flexion decreases the angle between bones (at the wrist, palm toward forearm). Extension increases it (back of hand toward forearm).

PROXIMAL

Proximal / Distal — closer to the body's center vs. farther away. Critical for locating injuries accurately.

RADIUS/ULNA

Forearm Bones — the two parallel bones. Radius = thumb side. Ulna = pinky side. Both fractured in Jackson's case.

DORSAL

Dorsal / Volar — back of the hand vs. palm side. Swelling patterns help localize injuries.

Your Journey

What to expect at your visits

Physical therapy follows a structured progression. Here's what a patient's experience looks like — from evaluation to return-to-sport clearance.

1Weeks 1 to 8

Immobilization

After surgery the cast or splint stays on for eight weeks, before PT begins. Only gentle finger and shoulder movement is allowed while the bones start to knit.

Cast or splintFinger movementShoulder movement
2Weeks 8 to 12

Active PT

Your first visit records baseline range of motion with a goniometer. Sessions then focus on gentle wrist, forearm, elbow and shoulder movement, plus exercises at home twice a day. Visits run 2 to 3 times a week, tapering to once a week. Flexibility usually returns within a few weeks.

ROM baselineGentle ROMHome program
3Months 3 to 6

Strengthening

Once your surgeon confirms the bones have joined, we add resistance and pressure exercises for the FCU, FCR, ECRL, brachialis, triceps (medial head) and deltoid. A hand therapist rebuilds fine motor skills for writing and typing. Strength takes longer to return than flexibility.

ResistancePressure workHand therapy
4About month 6

Return to sport

Supervised court drills can start at week 12. Full basketball, including overhead reach, catching, dribbling and shooting, returns only when the surgeon clears it, around month 6.

Questions

Do I need a referral?

It depends on your state and your insurance plan. Some plans require a doctor's referral before PT. We will check when you book.

How long will it take?

It depends on the injury. Jackson's both-bone forearm fracture needed 8 weeks in a cast or splint, active PT from week 8 to week 12, and strengthening through month 6. We give you a timeline after your first visit.

Can my team or school partner with you?

We are setting up team and school screening programs. See team and school partnerships.
