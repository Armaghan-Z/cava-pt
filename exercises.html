Anatomy & Physiology

Forearm musculature — Maniken model

These six muscles are the ones Jackson's exercises target. The team built each one on the Maniken model.

FCU
Wrist flexion

Flexor carpi ulnaris

Region: front of the forearm, pinky side. Starts at the medial epicondyle of the humerus and the back of the ulna; attaches to the pisiform, hamate and 5th metacarpal.

Action: flexes the wrist and tilts it toward the pinky side (ulnar deviation).

FCR
Wrist flexion

Flexor carpi radialis

Region: front of the forearm, middle. Starts at the medial epicondyle of the humerus; attaches to the base of the 2nd and 3rd metacarpals.

Action: flexes the wrist and helps tilt it toward the thumb side (radial deviation).

ECRL
Wrist extension, radial deviation

Extensor carpi radialis longus

Region: back of the forearm, thumb side. Starts at the lateral supracondylar ridge of the humerus; attaches to the back of the 2nd metacarpal.

Action: extends the wrist and tilts it toward the thumb side (radial deviation).

BR
Elbow flexion

Brachialis

Region: front of the upper arm, under the biceps. Starts on the lower front of the humerus; attaches to the coronoid process and tuberosity of the ulna.

Action: the main muscle that bends (flexes) the elbow.

TB
Elbow extension

Triceps brachii (medial head)

Region: back of the upper arm, the deepest head. Starts on the back of the humerus below the radial groove; attaches to the olecranon of the ulna.

Action: straightens (extends) the elbow.

DEL
Shoulder

Deltoid

Region: the cap of the shoulder. Starts on the clavicle, acromion and spine of the scapula; attaches to the deltoid tuberosity of the humerus.

Action: raises the arm. Front fibers flex the shoulder, middle fibers abduct it, back fibers extend it.

Surgical Hardware

ORIF of the radius and ulna

Jackson sustained a both-bone fracture of the right (dominant) forearm, which required Open Reduction and Internal Fixation (ORIF) surgery. The fracture is in the proximal third of the right forearm and affects the lateral bone (radius) and the medial bone (ulna). Metal plates and screws were inserted to hold the bones while they heal.

In plain English: both bones in Jackson's forearm broke and slipped out of line. Surgeons moved them back and held them together with a metal plate and screws, like a splint on the inside.

The hardware remains in place long-term and provides a stable scaffold for progressive loading during rehabilitation. Exercise selection must avoid excessive torque forces at the fixation sites, particularly during the first 4 weeks.

  • Radius: lateral forearm bone, primary load-bearer
  • Ulna: medial forearm bone, stabilizes rotation
  • PRUJ: proximal radioulnar joint, near the elbow, where the radial head turns against the ulna
  • DRUJ: distal radioulnar joint, at the wrist. Works with the PRUJ to allow pronation and supination
  • Interosseous membrane: a thin, strong sheet of tissue that links the radius and ulna and passes force between them
The Protocol

Eight exercises by phase

Exercises are listed in the order they start. Week numbers count from surgery; PT begins at week 8. Stop immediately if pain or swelling occurs.

1
Immobilization and active PT · Weeks 1 to 12

Finger fist-making

Keeps the fingers moving while the wrist is protected.

Starting position

Sit with the forearm resting on a pillow or table. During weeks 1 to 8 the cast or splint stays on.

Movement

Slowly curl the fingers into a gentle fist, then open the hand and straighten the fingers fully.

Finish

Relax the hand flat. Repeat 5 to 6 times through the day.

Precaution

Stop immediately if pain or swelling occurs. Movement should feel like a gentle stretch, never sharp pain.

Prescription
  • MusclesFinger flexors and extensors
  • RepsTBD by PT
  • Frequency5–6× a day
Modifications
  • Full fist hurts: curl only as far as comfortable
2
Active PT · Weeks 8 to 12

Forearm supination and pronation

Turning the palm up and down with the elbow bent to 90 degrees.

Starting position

Sit with the elbow bent to 90 degrees and tucked at your side, forearm supported on a table, thumb pointing up.

Movement

Slowly turn the palm up (supination), hold for 3 seconds, then turn it down (pronation) and hold for 3 seconds. Keep the elbow still. The other hand may guide gently.

Finish

Return to thumb up and rest for 60 seconds between sets.

Precaution

Stop immediately if pain or swelling occurs. Stay in the pain-free part of the motion. Swelling, warmth or more pain afterward means the dose was too high: reduce reps and tell your PT.

Prescription
  • Sets2–3
  • Reps10–15
  • Hold3 sec
  • Rest60 sec
  • Frequency2× daily
Modifications
  • Pain: make the turn smaller by about 10 degrees
  • More swelling: ice for 10 minutes afterward
3
Active PT · Weeks 8 to 12, then strengthening · Months 3 to 6

Wrist flexion and extension

Flexors (FCU, FCR) bend the wrist; the ECRL extends it.

Starting position

Sit with the forearm resting on a table and the hand hanging just off the edge, palm up for flexion. No weight during weeks 8 to 12.

Movement

Curl the wrist up through the comfortable range (FCU and FCR), hold about 6 seconds, then lower slowly. Flip to palm down and lift the back of the hand (ECRL), hold about 6 seconds, and lower slowly. Do flexion and extension as separate sets. The other hand may assist.

Finish

Finish with slow wrist circles in each direction, then rest the hand flat.

Strengthening, months 3 to 6

Only after the surgeon confirms bone union, add wrist curls and wrist extensions with a 1 lb dumbbell. Tempo: 1 second up, 1 second hold, 3 seconds down. Add 0.5 lb only when 3 sets of 15 feel pain-free with good form for two sessions in a row. Use loads that tire the muscle without pain.

Eccentric phase: the slow lowering part of an exercise, which helps tendons rebuild strength.

Precaution

Stop immediately if pain or swelling occurs.

Prescription
  • Sets2 (then 2–3)
  • Reps10–12 (then 10–15)
  • HoldAbout 6 sec
  • LoadNone, then 1–3 lb
  • Frequency2× daily, then 3–4× weekly
Modifications
  • Assist the movement with the other hand
  • Weak grip: use Theraputty instead of a dumbbell
  • Pain at end range: make the movement about 15 degrees smaller
4
Active PT · Weeks 8 to 12, weight from month 3

Radial deviation

Tilting the hand toward the thumb with the ECRL and FCR, building toward 20 degrees.

Starting position

Rest the forearm on a table, thumb pointing up, with the hand just past the edge.

Movement

Tilt the hand up toward the thumb side without lifting the forearm, then lower slowly back to neutral.

Finish

Rest the hand. The goal is 20 degrees of radial deviation by week 10, from 15 degrees at baseline.

Adding weight

Start with no weight. Hold a light weight only after the surgeon confirms bone union, from month 3.

Precaution

Stop immediately if pain or swelling occurs.

Prescription
  • MusclesECRL, FCR
  • Sets2
  • Reps10–12
  • LoadNone, then light weight
  • Frequency2× daily
Modifications
  • Pain: make the tilt smaller
5
Active PT · Weeks 8 to 12

Controlled elbow flexion and extension

Brachialis bends the elbow; the triceps medial head straightens it.

Starting position

Sit tall with the arm at your side and the palm facing in.

Movement

Slowly bend the elbow to bring the hand toward the shoulder (brachialis), then slowly straighten it all the way (triceps, medial head).

Finish

Let the arm rest straight at your side between sets.

Precaution

Stop immediately if pain or swelling occurs.

Prescription
  • Sets2
  • Reps10
  • Frequency2× daily
Modifications
  • Rest the arm on a table and slide it without lifting
6
Active PT · Weeks 8 to 12

Seated deltoid arm raises

Keeps the shoulder strong for shooting and overhead reach.

Starting position

Sit tall in a chair with the arms relaxed at your sides.

Movement

Slowly raise the arm to shoulder height, keeping the elbow straight but not locked.

Finish

Lower the arm slowly back to your side.

Precaution

Stop immediately if pain or swelling occurs.

Prescription
  • MuscleDeltoid
  • Sets3
  • Reps8–10
  • Frequency1× daily
Modifications
  • Raise the arm only halfway
7
Strengthening · Months 3 to 6

Wall weight shifts

Pressure exercise that teaches the wrist to accept body weight again.

Starting position

Stand facing a wall at arm's length. Place the palm flat on the wall at shoulder height, elbow slightly bent.

Movement

Slowly shift your body weight toward the hand, only as far as is comfortable, then shift back.

Finish

Step back from the wall and relax the hand.

Precaution

Start only after the surgeon confirms bone union. Stop immediately if pain or swelling occurs.

Prescription
  • SetsTBD by PT
  • RepsTBD by PT
  • Frequency3–4× weekly
Modifications
  • Lean less weight into the wall
8
Strengthening · Months 3 to 6

Resistance band diagonal chop

A sport-style pattern that links the forearm, shoulder and core.

Setup

Anchor a light resistance band at shoulder height. Stand side-on to the anchor, feet shoulder-width apart, holding the band with both hands and reaching across the body toward the anchor.

Movement

Pull the band diagonally down and across the body in a chopping motion. The forearm, wrist, shoulder and core work together, like a chest pass or crossover dribble.

Finish

Return slowly to the start. Move to a medium band only when your PT says so.

Precaution

Start only after the surgeon confirms bone union. Medicine-ball variants are allowed only after the surgeon confirms union, with the athletic trainer supervising. Stop immediately if pain or swelling occurs.

Prescription
  • Sets2–3
  • Reps10 each side
  • BandLight, then medium
  • Rest90 sec
  • Frequency3–4× weekly
Modifications
  • Low strength: seated version with a lighter band
  • Advanced: add a controlled stop at the end of the motion

Anatomical Language

Directional terms & reference

Standard anatomical directional terms used throughout this program, referenced from the anatomical position (standing upright, palms forward).

Anterior01

Toward the front of the body. The flexor compartment of the forearm is on the anterior side.

Posterior02

Toward the back of the body. Extensor muscles run along the posterior forearm.

Medial03

Toward the midline. The ulna is the medial bone of the forearm.

Lateral04

Away from the midline. The radius is the lateral bone of the forearm.

Proximal05

Closer to the origin (torso). The elbow joint is proximal to the wrist.

Distal06

Farther from the origin. The wrist is distal to the elbow in the forearm.

Supination07

Forearm rotation that turns the palm up when the elbow is bent, like holding a bowl of soup.

Pronation08

Forearm rotation that turns the palm down when the elbow is bent, like pouring out the soup.

Dorsal09

The back surface of the hand or wrist. Extensor tendons run along the dorsal surface.

Volar / Palmar10

The front surface of the hand. Flexor tendons and the carpal tunnel are volar structures.

Radial Deviation11

Wrist movement toward the thumb side (radius). Limited by the radiocarpal ligaments.

Ulnar Deviation12

Wrist movement toward the pinky side (ulna). Important for writing and racket sports.
