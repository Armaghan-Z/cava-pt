Care & Rehabilitation Plan

Goals for the Patient

What Jackson wants to achieve

Jackson communicated four clear outcomes at his initial consultation. Every element of the care plan was designed to meet these specific goals.

01

Return to basketball

Jackson's main goal is to return to competitive basketball, including dunking and shooting, once his surgeon clears him.

02

Daily hand function

Regain the ability to write, type, dress and drive, all restricted after surgery. Fine motor control matters for his schoolwork during recovery.

03

ROM goals

Increase right wrist flexion from 50 to 60 degrees by week 12 and radial deviation from 15 to 20 degrees by week 10, then reach 75 to 80 degrees of flexion by month 6. These motions matter for catching, dribbling and shooting.

04

Confidence

Jackson is anxious about re-injury, especially going up for dunks. Regaining confidence for high-impact play is as important as the physical recovery.

SMART goals

  • Flexion: increase right wrist flexion from 50 degrees to 60 degrees within 4 weeks, measured with a goniometer at every visit.
  • Radial deviation: increase right wrist radial deviation from 15 degrees to 20 degrees within 2 weeks, measured with a goniometer at every visit.
  • Long term: reach 75 to 80 degrees of wrist flexion by the end of strengthening (month 6) and return to basketball once the surgeon clears him.
  • Return to play: surgeon clearance plus at least 90 percent grip-strength symmetry on dynamometry.

"Within 2 weeks" and "within 4 weeks" count from the first PT visit at week 8, so these goals fall at week 10 and week 12.

Recovery Timeline

Four recovery phases

Jackson's plan has four phases, and each must be met before the next begins. Flexibility returns within a few weeks of starting PT. Strengthening takes much longer because the fracture was severe, with major displacement and soft-tissue loss, so full return to basketball takes time.

Multidisciplinary Team

Jackson's care team

A single provider rarely has all the expertise recovery requires. Three specialists each address a different dimension of rehabilitation.

Primary Oversight

Orthopedic Surgeon

Checks bone healing with follow-up X-rays at weeks 3, 6 and 12 after surgery and confirms the hardware is secure. Strengthening starts only after the surgeon confirms union at the preceding check-in (week 12). Gives final return-to-sport clearance, around month 6. All major loading changes need the surgeon's sign-off.

Fine Motor Recovery

Occupational / Hand Therapist

Rebuilds fine motor skills for daily academic tasks: handwriting, typing, using a phone, and dressing. Provides a custom wrist orthotic for study sessions. Critical for Jackson's return to coursework weeks before full PT completion.

Sport Return

Athletic Trainer / Strength Coach

Designs the basketball-specific return-to-sport protocol for Phases 3 and 4: progressive on-court drills that rebuild dribbling, passing, shooting, and overhead catch mechanics. Monitors for compensation patterns that could increase re-injury risk.

Beyond Exercise

Additional strategies & interventions

Exercise alone doesn't complete recovery. These evidence-supported interventions address lifestyle, psychological, and recovery-technology dimensions of Jackson's plan.

Assistive Device

Custom Wrist Orthotic

A hand therapist-fitted orthotic holds the wrist in a neutral position during studying and typing, protecting ORIF hardware from rotational stress. Allows Jackson to return to coursework weeks earlier than without it.

Mental Health

Sports Psychology Coaching

Cognitive-behavioral strategies for kinesiophobia (fear of movement) are introduced during active PT. Visualization, graded exposure to high-impact scenarios, and confidence-building progressions ensure mental readiness matches physical readiness at return to play.

Recovery Modality

Contrast Hydrotherapy

Alternating warm (38°C) and cool (15°C) water immersion 3× weekly during Phases 1–2. Cycles vasoconstriction and vasodilation to flush metabolic waste and promote nutrient delivery to healing bone and muscle tissue.

Recovery Modality

Low-Level Laser Therapy

Applied at the fracture sites at select in-office visits. Uses 630–1000nm wavelengths shown in research to reduce inflammation and accelerate cellular repair — particularly beneficial near ORIF hardware where direct massage is contraindicated.
