Jackson's Care Plan

Built for Jackson Sport Basketball Wrist flexion now 50° Radial deviation now 15°
Goals

Jackson wants to regain forearm strength and range of motion. He wants to be able to play basketball in the future. In the wrist he wants to get a flexion range of motion of 60 degrees. Additionally, he wants to get a radial range of motion of 20 degrees.

Short-term goals
  • By week 2: Reach 20 degrees of radial deviation so he can tilt his wrist to dribble.
  • By week 4: Reach 60 degrees of wrist flexion so he can write, type and drive without stiffness.
Long-term goals
  • By month 6: Reach 75 to 80 degrees of wrist flexion for a full follow-through on his shot.
  • After surgeon clearance: Return to full-contact basketball and finish the dunk that injured him.

Weeks ~ 1-6

Range of motion · In clinic 3-4× a week, at home on off days

Flexor carpi ulnaris · Flexor carpi radialis · Extensor carpi radialis longus

Wrist Flexion Off of a Table

Rest forearm on a table with metacarpals and anything more distal hanging off the table with the palm facing down. Bend your wrist up and down slowly, holding each position for around 6 seconds. Repeat for about 8-12 reps at a time

Sets × reps
2-3 sets of 8-12 reps, 6 second hold
Where
In clinic and at home
Goal
Wrist flexion 50° → 60° by week 4
Modification
Too stiff? Use the left hand to gently guide the wrist

Flexor carpi ulnaris · Flexor carpi radialis · Extensor carpi radialis longus

Wrist circles

Slow gentle wrist circles clockwise and counterclockwise

Sets × reps
2 sets of 10 circles each way
Where
At home, daily warm-up
Goal
Smooth, pain-free circles in both directions
Modification
Make the circles smaller if the wrist feels tight

Forearm rotators · Brachialis

Forearm supination and pronation

Forearm supination and pronation: Tuck in arm close to side at a 90 degree angle. Slowly rotate forearm back and forth as the palm faces up and down

Sets × reps
2-3 sets of 10-15 reps
Where
In clinic and at home
Goal
Turn palm fully up and fully down. Target degrees: TBD by PT
Modification
Hold a light hammer or pen to help guide the turn
Video

Extensor carpi radialis longus

Radial deviation with a light weight Added

Sets × reps
2-3 sets of 10-15 reps, 1-2 lb
Where
In clinic, then at home once form is good
Goal
Radial deviation 15° → 20° by week 2
Modification
No weight at first; just tilt the hand toward the thumb

Flexor carpi ulnaris · Flexor carpi radialis

Grip squeeze with putty Added

Sets × reps
2 sets of 15 squeezes, 3 second hold
Where
At home
Goal
Grip strength closer to the left hand each week
Modification
Start with soft putty and move to firmer putty

Weeks ~6 - 12

Resistance · In clinic 3-4× a week, at home on off days

Flexor carpi ulnaris · Flexor carpi radialis · Extensor carpi radialis longus

Resistance Exercises

Wrist curls with increasing resistance over time. Wrist extensions

Sets × reps
2-3 sets of 10-15 reps
Where
In clinic with dumbbells, at home with a resistance band
Goal
Add weight only when 15 reps feel easy
Modification
Use a band or a water bottle instead of a dumbbell
Video
Wrist flexion & extension with dumbbell

Triceps brachii medial head · Deltoid · Wrist extensors

Pressure exercises

Put palm flat on a wall and slowly increase body weight onto the wrists

Sets × reps
2-3 sets of 10 slow leans, 5 second hold
Where
At home and in clinic
Goal
Hold body weight on the wrist with no pain
Modification
Stand closer to the wall to put less weight through the wrist

Weeks ~12+

Return to sport · Only after surgeon clearance

Ball handling

2-3 sets of 30 seconds, stationary then moving.

Diagonal band chop and overhead catching

2-3 sets of 10-12 each side. Video

Progressive shooting

Form shots close in, then free throws, then jump shots. Dunks last.

At home

On off days

  • Wrist flexion off a table
  • Wrist circles
  • Supination and pronation
  • Grip squeeze with putty
  • Wall pressure (weeks ~6-12)
In clinic

With the PT

What to expect/frequency of exercises:

How often, how hard

  • Sessions of PT every other day around 3-4 times a week
  • Stop doing exercises immediately if pain or swelling occurs
  • 2-3 sets for 10-15 reps based on ability that day for each exercise
  • Use weights and resistance that makes the muscles/arms feel tired but doesn't hurt
  • Strengthening will take time as the fracture was severe with severe amounts of displacement, meaning there is a major loss in soft tissue near and on the fracture lines thus it will takes lots of time to return back to full function especially playing basketball again
  • Flexibility should return in a few weeks after starting PT
