Jackson's Story

01 · The injury

Rim Check

Jackson was competing in an energetic pickup basketball game when he went up for a dunk and caught his right arm on the rim. The fall sent his full body weight through the right forearm, which was trapped in the rim, resulting in a complete fracture of both the radius and ulna.

02 · Emergency department

At GNMH's Emergency Department, anteroposterior and lateral X-rays confirmed a displaced proximal forearm fracture

03 · Physical exam

Physical exam found swelling over the dorsal hand and fingers, but sensation, circulation, and active finger extension remained fully intact. Neurovascular status was intact, indicating no major nerve or blood vessel damage.

  • Sensation intact
  • Circulation intact
  • Finger extension intact
04 · Surgery

Bolted Back Together

Eight weeks before his first physical therapy visit, Jackson underwent Open Reduction and Internal Fixation (ORIF). Both fractured bones were moved back into alignment and secured with metal plates and screws. A cock-up plaster splint was applied after surgery and remained in place throughout the immobilization period (weeks 1 to 8) to protect the hardware and keep the wrist in position. During those weeks, he could only gently move his fingers and shoulder.

Before: broken radius and ulnaAfter: two plates and screws

Jackson's Goals #23 · Right arm

  1. Jackson wanted to return to basketball and play at his full capacity. One of his personal goals was to make the dunk that injured him.

  2. Jackson wanted to restore his daily hand functions, and be able to write, type, and drive

  3. ROM goals: wrist flexion from 50 to 60 degrees and radial deviation from 15 to 20 degrees, then 75 to 80 degrees of flexion by month 6.

  4. Jackson wanted to regain his confidence and overcome his anxiety risking re-injury at rim. He wanted his competitive edge back. Kinesiophobia is a fear of moving because you're afraid of getting hurt again. It's common and treatable.

05 · Rehab

Jackson's Rehabilitation Journey

Jackson's bones are now fully healed after a successful orthopedic repair that led to two months of healing. Now Jackson wants to regain all the range of motion he lost in his forearm after the fracture and long periods of no movement. This requires PT, where we have made a plan to restore motion

Jackson's Care Network

OS

Jackson's care plan will also involve an orthopedic surgeon to monitor surgical hardware and bone healing.

HT

An occupational therapist or certified hand therapist will help him rebuild fine motor skills for daily activities.

MP

Throughout his recovery, Jackson will work closely with a Certified Mental Performance Consultant to overcome his Kinesiophobia

AT

Later in his recovery, an athletic trainer or strength coach can help him condition safely for his return to basketball.

Additional Interventions
As co-founder Vasanth says, "The best way to attack the rim is from multiple angles."

Beyond exercise, Jackson will have access to our custom wrist orthotics and ergonomic assistive devices to protect his arm during daily activities like typing or studying.

Additionally, advanced recovery interventions such as high-intensity contrast hydrotherapy or low-level laser therapy will be used to accelerate soft tissue healing and control inflammation.
